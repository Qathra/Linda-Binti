CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) remain major social and human rights challenges affecting millions of women and girls globally. In Kenya, despite government interventions, legal frameworks, and awareness campaigns, many cases of GBV and FGM continue to occur, especially in vulnerable and marginalized communities. Victims often face barriers such as fear, stigma, lack of immediate support, and limited access to reporting mechanisms.
Advancements in Information and Communication Technology (ICT), Artificial Intelligence (AI), mobile computing, and geolocation technologies have created opportunities for developing innovative solutions that enhance personal safety and support vulnerable populations. Mobile applications have increasingly been used for emergency response, incident reporting, and access to support services.
Linda Binti is proposed as a mobile and web-based safety system that combines emergency alert mechanisms, AI-powered counseling support, secure incident reporting, and data visualization tools. The system is designed to provide discreet access to assistance for individuals at risk of GBV and FGM while enabling organizations to monitor trends and coordinate interventions effectively.
The proposed solution leverages AI chatbot technology, GPS tracking, secure databases, and web-based dashboards to improve reporting, response, and prevention efforts. Through the integration of these technologies, the system seeks to contribute to the fight against GBV and FGM in Kenya.
1.2 Problem Statement
Despite the existence of helplines, awareness campaigns, and protection agencies, many victims of GBV and FGM are unable to seek help promptly due to fear of retaliation, social stigma, lack of privacy, and limited access to emergency support systems. Traditional reporting mechanisms are often visible, time-consuming, or inaccessible during emergencies.
Additionally, organizations working to combat GBV and FGM frequently encounter challenges in collecting accurate data, identifying high-risk regions, and responding effectively to emerging incidents. Existing systems often lack real-time reporting, location tracking, and AI-assisted analysis capabilities.
Therefore, there is a need for a secure, discreet, and intelligent system that enables victims to request assistance quickly, access confidential guidance, report incidents safely, and support organizations in monitoring and preventing GBV and FGM cases.

1.6 Scope and Delimitation
1.6.1 Scope of the study
This study focuses on the design and development of Linda Binti, an AI-powered mobile and web-based safety platform for the prevention and response to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM). The proposed system consists of a mobile application that enables users to register, manage emergency contacts, activate a discreet SOS alert using a secret code, access an AI-powered chatbot for confidential guidance and support, and report GBV and FGM incidents securely. The system also incorporates GPS technology to share the user’s location with trusted emergency contacts during emergencies. In addition, the study includes the development of a web-based dashboard that enables authorized organizations, such as NGOs and protection agencies, to monitor reported incidents, visualize trends, and identify high-risk areas through AI-assisted data analysis. The study is limited to the design, development, and evaluation of a prototype system and does not include nationwide deployment.

This study is limited to the prevention and response to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) within Kenya and does not address other forms of crime or emergency situations. The proposed system is intended to complement existing support services by facilitating emergency reporting, providing AI-based guidance, and improving communication between users and authorized organizations; it does not replace professional counseling, healthcare services, law enforcement, or legal intervention. In addition, some system features, such as GPS location sharing and real-time notifications, depend on internet connectivity, GPS availability, and access to compatible mobile devices. Due to time and resource constraints, the study focuses on the development and testing of a functional prototype rather than a fully deployed national system.

2.2 Review of existing literature
2.2.1 Gender-Based Violence (GBV)
Gender-Based Violence (GBV) remains one of the most widespread human rights and public health challenges affecting women and girls across the world. It includes physical, sexual, emotional, psychological, and economic violence that occurs because of unequal power relations and gender inequality. GBV affects individuals of all ages and backgrounds, leading to long-term physical injuries, mental health problems, reduced educational opportunities, and negative socio-economic outcomes for survivors (UN Women, 2024).
According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2024), approximately one in three women globally experiences physical or sexual violence during her lifetime, with most incidents being committed by intimate partners. Despite national laws, awareness campaigns, and community interventions, many GBV cases remain unreported because victims fear retaliation, social stigma, discrimination, and lack of confidence in existing reporting mechanisms (WHO, 2024).
In Kenya, Gender-Based Violence continues to be a major concern despite the implementation of legal frameworks and protection policies. Reports published by UN Women (2024) indicate that survivors often experience difficulties in accessing timely medical care, legal assistance, psychosocial support, and emergency protection services. Challenges such as limited reporting channels, fear of exposure, cultural beliefs, and inadequate coordination among service providers continue to affect effective prevention and response efforts.
The increasing availability of smartphones and internet connectivity has created opportunities for using digital technologies to improve GBV prevention and response. Mobile applications, emergency alert systems, online counseling platforms, and Artificial Intelligence-based support tools are increasingly being explored as mechanisms for providing confidential reporting, immediate assistance, and access to support services. These technologies have the potential to reduce reporting barriers, improve communication between survivors and support organizations, and enhance emergency response (UNICEF, 2023).
Although several digital solutions have been introduced to support survivors of Gender-Based Violence, many existing systems primarily focus on reporting incidents after they have occurred. Few systems integrate discreet emergency activation, Artificial Intelligence-powered guidance, location sharing, and organizational monitoring into a single platform. This limitation highlights the need for an integrated mobile and web-based safety platform such as Linda Binti that combines emergency assistance, AI-based support, secure reporting, and organizational monitoring for improved prevention and response to GBV.

2.2.2 Female Genital Mutilation (FGM)
Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) is recognized as one of the most harmful traditional practices affecting women and girls worldwide. According to UNICEF (2025), FGM involves the partial or total removal of the external female genitalia or other injuries to the female genital organs for non-medical reasons. The practice violates the human rights of women and girls and has no known health benefits. Instead, it exposes victims to severe physical pain, excessive bleeding, infections, complications during childbirth, psychological trauma, and, in some cases, death. Although many countries have enacted laws prohibiting FGM, the practice continues due to cultural beliefs, social pressure, and gender inequality.
UNICEF (2025) reports that millions of girls and women have undergone FGM globally, with the practice remaining common in several African countries, including Kenya. In some communities, FGM is viewed as a rite of passage into womanhood or a requirement for marriage. These beliefs have made it difficult to eliminate the practice despite continuous awareness campaigns, legal enforcement, and community education programmes. Many girls remain at risk because families often perform FGM in secrecy to avoid legal consequences.
The continued occurrence of FGM demonstrates the importance of strengthening prevention and response mechanisms through technology. According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2024), early reporting, community awareness, and timely intervention are essential in protecting girls who are at risk. However, fear of stigma, family pressure, and concerns about confidentiality discourage many victims and witnesses from reporting suspected cases. As a result, there is a need for secure and confidential reporting platforms that enable individuals to seek assistance without exposing themselves to danger.
Information and Communication Technology (ICT) provides an opportunity to improve the prevention and response to FGM through mobile applications, location-based services, and Artificial Intelligence. Mobile safety applications can enable users to request emergency assistance, report suspected cases confidentially, and receive guidance on available support services. Artificial Intelligence can further improve these systems by providing instant responses through chatbots, identifying patterns from reported incidents, and supporting organizations in allocating resources to high-risk areas. These technologies can complement existing government and community initiatives by improving accessibility, confidentiality, and response efficiency.
Despite these technological advancements, many existing digital solutions primarily focus on reporting incidents after they have occurred and provide limited support for early prevention. Few systems combine discreet emergency activation, AI-powered guidance, GPS location sharing, and organizational monitoring within a single platform. This limitation creates a gap that the proposed Linda Binti system seeks to address by integrating these features into one mobile and web-based safety platform designed specifically for the prevention and response to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM).

2.2.3 Artificial Intelligence Chatbots in GBV and FGM Support
Artificial Intelligence (AI) chatbots have become increasingly important in providing digital support services, particularly in situations where users require confidential guidance and immediate access to information. AI chatbots are computer programs that use Artificial Intelligence and Natural Language Processing (NLP) to interact with users through text or voice conversations. Unlike traditional chat systems that rely on predefined responses, AI chatbots are capable of understanding user questions and providing relevant guidance based on available knowledge.
In the context of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM), AI chatbots provide a safe and confidential environment where individuals can seek information without fear of stigma or discrimination. Survivors and individuals at risk may hesitate to disclose their experiences to family members, friends, or authorities due to fear, trauma, or social pressure. AI chatbots help bridge this gap by offering immediate guidance, safety information, referral services, and emotional support while maintaining user privacy.
Several humanitarian organizations have adopted AI-powered conversational technologies to improve access to support services. These systems provide information about available services, explain users’ rights, recommend safety measures, and direct individuals to appropriate organizations where professional assistance can be obtained. Although AI chatbots cannot replace trained counselors or healthcare professionals, they complement existing support services by ensuring that assistance is available at any time.
For the proposed Linda Binti platform, the AI chatbot will provide confidential guidance to individuals who fear becoming victims of GBV or FGM. The chatbot will answer frequently asked questions, provide information on available support services, recommend safety measures, and encourage users to seek professional assistance whenever necessary. This functionality is expected to improve access to information while promoting user confidentiality and timely intervention.
Despite their advantages, AI chatbots have certain limitations. They may not fully understand complex emotional situations, cultural expressions, or highly sensitive conversations. Consequently, AI-generated responses should serve as supportive guidance rather than professional medical, psychological, or legal advice. Human intervention remains essential in situations requiring specialized care or emergency response.

Mobile-based safety systems allow users to communicate during emergency situations through features such as alert notifications, location sharing, and digital reporting mechanisms. These technologies have created new opportunities for improving access to support services for individuals experiencing Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) (World Health Organization [WHO], 2021).
Emergency alert systems are commonly used to provide rapid communication between individuals in danger and trusted contacts or response organizations. The integration of location-based technologies such as GPS can support emergency response by providing information that may assist in locating individuals during critical situations. Digital platforms have also contributed to improving awareness, reporting, and coordination efforts in addressing violence against women and girls (UN Women, 2023).

Most do not provide a complete protection system that combines discreet emergency activation, confidential AI-based guidance, incident reporting, and organizational decision-support tools. In addition, many existing solutions are not specifically designed to address the unique challenges associated with GBV and FGM, where victims may require privacy, secrecy, and immediate support due to fear of stigma or retaliation.

The proposed Linda Binti system seeks to address this gap by providing a discreet mobile application with secret emergency activation, AI-powered chatbot support, secure incident reporting, and a web-based monitoring platform for authorized organizations.

2.3 Theory and Citation
2.3.1 Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) was introduced by Davis (1989) to explain how users accept and adopt new technologies. The model suggests that two main factors influence users’ acceptance of a system: perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. Perceived usefulness refers to the extent to which a user believes that a technology will improve their ability to achieve a specific goal, while perceived ease of use refers to how simple and convenient the technology is to operate.
In relation to Linda Binti, TAM supports the development of a user-friendly mobile application that enables individuals at risk of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) to easily access emergency support, use the AI chatbot, and activate SOS alerts. Since users may need to interact with the application during stressful situations, simplicity, accessibility, and reliability are important factors that influence acceptance of the system.
The theory is therefore relevant to this study because the success of Linda Binti depends on whether vulnerable individuals can easily understand, trust, and use the application when seeking support.
(Davis, 1989)
2.3.2 Protection Motivation Theory (PMT)
Protection Motivation Theory (PMT) was developed by Rogers (1975) to explain how individuals respond to threats and decide to take protective actions. The theory explains that people’s behaviour is influenced by their perception of a threat, their understanding of the severity of the threat, and their belief that they are capable of taking effective action to protect themselves.
In the context of Linda Binti, PMT is relevant because individuals facing risks related to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) may need immediate support and a safe way to respond to threats. The system provides protective mechanisms such as discreet SOS activation, GPS-based location sharing, AI guidance, and access to support resources, which can encourage users to take action when they feel threatened.
The theory supports the idea that providing accessible and reliable digital safety tools can improve individuals’ ability to respond to risks and seek assistance during emergency situations. Therefore, PMT provides a foundation for understanding the role of technology in supporting prevention and response efforts against GBV and FGM.
(Rogers, 1975)
